## Part I: Walking Naked Among Strangers — Or How I Learned to Stop Worrying About God (`R03_WNA`)

I set out on this trip because Tracey and I have been dealing with a lot. I didn't know how long I would be gone but promised I would be back when I found what I was looking for. And because she understands me, crazy, fallible me, she let me go and said she would be back. I have done this before, and I didn't realize it until tonight, that I don't come back until I feel and know that I can't live without her. I wandered the earth before her, never knowing what I was looking for, no great plan, no great ambition. Just Forrest Gumping the hell out of life. And somehow, I always land on my feet, bloody, broken, but still alive. Then I met her, she just showed up at my house one night, crying. And I just knew, knew that I would trade my soul for another smile. And it scared the shit out of me. It still does, every day. To have someone hold such power over you is incredible and frightening.

So maybe, out here on the road I remember that feeling. All I know is that when it hits I go straight home, just have to. I took a month to make it to Northern California. It took four days, hailstorm, outrunning a thunderstorm for a day across the plains, and running past dark to pitch a tent the first place I could find. And to be honest, it wasn't planned, I just rode like I felt, no itinerary, just broad strokes.

I abhor interstates and what they have done to our country. But I also am in adoration at the magnificent technologies they have given birth to. But they make it impossible to get lost. Because everything is the same. Right down to the font choices. So I eschew the interstates in practice and principle. And I get lost.

By then it's dawn and I'm happily lost. Have barely slept or eaten in weeks. Drove all night. But ever since the army sleep is optional. So I'm alert and enjoying the back road morning air, windows down, alone with my thoughts. I have been quiet now for hours. The conversations don't seem to ever last the night. I think that's why campfires are the best for those times. Some nights you throw a log on. Stare quietly. And just know it's the last and start to think of sleep and tomorrow peacefully as you watch the embers.

Other nights you just keep feeding logs. For a while you think about your talks. Letting Grandpa know how much you liked going fishing with him. He always let me be myself. Delighted in my joy. He always ribbed me about how his other fishing buddies didn't want to mess up my perfect ropes when I wasn't out with him. Looking back I see the Navy pride in helping the Bosun run a tight ship. I knew everything's place on our ship. Right down to the lunches and every tool in the tackle box. My ropes were always clean, never jammed. The anchor and the ship's stores were mine. He ran the boat, and I helped it run.

And among the worst of the sins of the fisherman is the bird's nest. If you release the line too late with your finger, the force of the rig and bobber comes to a sudden halt, hook, lead, and bobber come flying back at the boat at high speeds. In an effort to leave the skipper free to perform his duties I have learned that I live to untangle things. Knotted things are easy. You just look at it a few different ways, then play cat's cradle for a while, and if you are patient and don't tug too hard, voilà. Bird's nest gone. Time to show the skipper. He's pleased, as proud as you've seen him. One less thing to keep him from his sacred duties. This deserves a feast. Lunch time. The best part of the day. My reward for a job well done: one diet caffeine-free Pepsi, gold label, slightly warm, tap first lightly before opening to avoid explosion. One slightly warm white bread mayo, cheddar cheese, and bologna—or braunschweiger if he had some—sandwich cut vertically. A feast well earned. Give me a tangled, gnarled mess of something and I know there's a sandwich at the end of it.

As I sit here along the banks of the Ohio, watching barges pass in the night. The calm. I can't help but think of the Bible verse about God leading you beside still waters. And I wonder: *Do the stars look back at me?*
